# P1 (Dany): "Soon OpenAI and Anthropic will make their subscription prices close to their API usage prices" — i.e. the flat coding subscription stops being a subsidy, which is what makes P2 ("until then, build a harness that can use cheaper models") a time-boxed hedge.

**Verdict:** premise holds with a narrower statement

## Strongest refutation

**Reading (a) — headline prices rise to API-equivalent — is refuted by 17 months of price history, and the movement has been in the opposite direction on BOTH ladders.**

| Plan                      | 2025                            | Today (2026-09-08)                                                                                                               | Direction                                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Claude Pro                | $20 (Apr 2025)                  | $20 / $17 annual                                                                                                                 | flat                                     |
| Claude Max 5x / 20x       | $100 / $200 (2025-04-09)        | $100 / $200                                                                                                                      | flat, 17 months                          |
| Team Premium              | $150 annual (Dec 2025 snapshot) | $100 annual / $125 monthly                                                                                                       | **down 33%**                             |
| ChatGPT Business Standard | $25 annual / $30 monthly        | $20 / $25 (cut 2026-04-02)                                                                                                       | **down $5**                              |
| ChatGPT tiers added       | Plus $20, Pro $200              | + **Go $8** (2026-01-16), + **Pro 5x $100** (2026-04-09), + **Business Premium $100/$125 with NO five-hour cap** (GA 2026-08-25) | **three new tiers, two of them cheaper** |

API list moved _down toward_ the plans rather than the plans moving up: Sonnet 5 held at $2/$10 with the scheduled 2026-09-01 rise to $3/$15 **cancelled**; GPT-5.6 Terra −20%, Luna −80% (2026-07-30); GPT-5.6 Sol −20% in / −33% out (2026-08-21); Fable 5.1's cache read is **0.025x** ($0.25/MTok) vs Fable 5's 0.1x ($1.00) — a 4x cut in the token category that is 90–97% of an agent loop.

**Reading (b) — allowance shrinks until value/price ≈ 1x — is not happening; net allowance expanded over the last 12 months.** Every tightening was reversed or softened once compute landed:

- 2025-08-28 weekly caps → 2026-03 peak-hour cut (~7% of users) → **2026-05-06 five-hour limits permanently DOUBLED and peak-hour reduction removed** (300 MW / 220,000 GPU SpaceX deal).
- Claude Code **+50% weekly** limits, extended five times (Jul 13 → Jul 19 → Aug 19 → Aug 31 → **Sep 13, 2026**); Anthropic on 2026-08-19: "We hope to make this a permanent change."
- OpenAI: Codex rate limits **doubled** on all paid plans at the Codex app launch; Codex given to **Free and Go**; one-off reset for all paid plans 2026-04-28; Plus 5-hour cap lifted 2026-07-12, restored 2026-08-25 with Pro $100/$200 **exempt "for the upcoming months."**

The 0.75x and 30x answers are the _same question on two populations_; that spread is the whole argument. It also explains why the instrument has always been **caps on the tail, not price on everyone**:

_Derived cost-to-serve (label: assumptions A-mix 60/30/10 Sonnet-5/Opus-5/Haiku-4.5, A-h cache hit 0.90, A-out output = 1.5% of processed input, A-days 22 active days/month, A-cost SemiAnalysis realized $0.99/MTok blended at 70% inference GM → lab cost ≈ $0.297/MTok):_

- Per 1M processed input + 15k output, `U·p_in·1.25 + C·p_cache + O·p_out`: Haiku 4.5 = 0.125+0.090+0.075 = **$0.290**; Sonnet 5 = 0.250+0.180+0.150 = **$0.580**; Opus 5 = 0.625+0.450+0.375 = **$1.450**; Fable 5.1 = 1.250+0.225+0.750 = **$2.225**; GPT-5.6 Sol = 0.500+0.360+0.300 = **$1.160**; GPT-6 Astra = 1.250+0.900+0.750 = **$2.900**. Blend = 0.6(0.580)+0.3(1.450)+0.1(0.290) = **$0.812/M processed**.
- Anthropic's stated $13/dev/active-day ÷ $0.812 = **16.0M processed tokens/day** → 352M/month → lab cost 352 × $0.297 = **$104.5/month**. Low (Sonnet-heavy, h=0.95, $0.465/M → 28.0M/day → 616M/mo) = **$183**; high (Opus-heavy, h=0.85, $1.7375/M → 7.5M/day → 165M/mo) = **$49**.
- **Max 20x gross margin on the median developer = (200 − 105)/200 = 48%** (range 8%–76%). Max 5x = (100 − 105)/100 = **−5%**, breakeven.
- On the viberank tail (7.25x the median list spend): 7.25 × $105 = **$761/month cost on a $200 plan = −280% margin.**

A plan that clears ~48% on the median and −280% on the tail gets **rate-limited at the tail**, which is exactly the observed 2025–2026 behaviour. There is no margin necessity forcing (a) or (b), and Anthropic booked its **first operating profit in Q2 2026 ($559M on $10.9B)** with these plans in force.

**The freshest datum kills the "day-one flagship" version of (b).** The thesis sheet named the decisive test: does the next flagship launch credits-only on Pro _and stay there_? Fable 5.1 launched **2026-09-01**. Seven days later, claude.com/pricing (two independent fetches) shows the Fable row as Pro = "Usage credits" but **Max 5x and Max 20x = "50% of weekly limits"** — the flagship is _rationed inside the plan_, not sold at list, to the tier a factory would actually buy. And Opus 5 (2026-07-24, $5/$25) shipped as "the new default model on Claude Max" six weeks after Fable 5 went credits-only: the mechanism by which plans stay generous is a **second-tier flagship at half price**, not a repriced plan.

**Reading (c) is the only one with a real instrument — and that instrument is currently switched off.** The Agent SDK / `claude -p` / GitHub Actions / ACP carve-out (announced 2026-05-13 for 2026-06-15: programmatic use leaves the plan pool and draws a monthly non-rollover credit metered at API list, sized _equal to the plan price_ — Pro $20, Max 5x $100, Max 20x $200) was **paused 2026-06-15 and is still paused today** (verified fresh 2026-09-08: "We're pausing the changes… For now, nothing has changed"), 85 days with no new date and a promise of advance notice. Note what the design itself concedes: **the plan price does not change**; only the metering boundary moves. That is not "subscriptions priced close to API" — it is "your robots are billed separately," which is a different claim with a different consequence.

## Steelman

The strongest honest case for P1, which I concede in full:

1. **Every marginal token is already at API list, everywhere.** Anthropic usage credits on Pro/Max/Max20x are "billed at standard API rates" (support 12429409). Enterprise is "$20/seat. Usage cost scales with model and task" — "Every token your team uses—in Chat, Claude Code, or Cowork—is billed at standard API rates on top of your seat cost," with legacy bundled-token plans force-migrated by 2026-03-08. OpenAI's Codex credits reproduce API list **exactly**: $0.04/credit × the rate card recovers all twelve current cells (Sol 100/10/500 → $4/$0.40/$20; Astra 250/25/1,250 → $10/$1/$50; Luna 5/0.5/30 → $0.20/$0.02/$1.20; Terra 50/5/300 → $2/$0.20/$12), anchored by OpenAI's own "$100 in workspace credits (2,500 credits)". The subscription's _unit of account already is the API price_.
2. **The top tier has fully converged.** Enterprise at both labs is seat-fee-plus-meter. Anthropic's is $20/seat + all tokens at list — a 1.0x plan by construction.
3. **The newest flagship is excluded from the cheap tier from day one and stays excluded.** Fable 5 went credits-only on Pro from 2026-07-20 after two backlash extensions; Fable 5.1 launched 2026-09-01 and Pro is _still_ "Usage credits" a week later. On Pro, reading (b) is already at 1.0x for the frontier model.
4. **The labs say so.** Altman (2026-03-11): "our business... is going to look like selling tokens... people buy it from us on a meter." Nick Turley: "having an unlimited plan is like having an unlimited electricity plan. It just doesn't make sense." Cherny: subscriptions "weren't built for the usage patterns of these third-party tools."
5. **The specific instrument aimed at Twilight was designed, published with per-plan numbers, and only paused** — not withdrawn, not disclaimed. It names ACP by implication and `claude -p` by name, and `--bare` is documented as becoming the default for `-p`. A pause is a delay, not a refutation.
6. **Structural pressure is real and unresolved.** Task token counts double every ~6 months (Ding); the 4.7+ tokenizer adds ~30% tokens per character; Anthropic's 2025 gross margin was cut 50% → ~40% on inference running 23% over plan; Amodei: "We tried to plan very well for a world of 10x growth per year. And yet we saw 80x."
7. **The reseller layer has already converged to 1.0x** (Cursor $20/$20, Cursor's own docs: "All Auto modes bill at the list price of the model each request is routed to"), and GitHub's flex credits are explicitly "designed to adapt as the economics of AI evolve" — a published dial for shrinking the subsidy.

## Narrower statement

**Not** "subscription prices move close to API prices." What the evidence supports is a **metering boundary that has moved and will keep moving, at a constant plan price**:

> Anthropic and OpenAI have already priced at API list every channel except one — overflow credits (both labs, since 2025), enterprise seats (Anthropic: $20/seat + all tokens at list; OpenAI Codex credits = list at $0.04/credit), third-party harnesses (Anthropic, since 2026-04-04), and the frontier model on the cheap tier (Fable 5/5.1 on Pro, since 2026-07-20). The one remaining subsidised programmatic channel — **`claude -p` / Agent SDK / ACP on a Pro/Max/Team plan** — was scheduled for API-list metering on 2026-06-15 and has been paused since, with advance notice promised. Meanwhile _interactive, individual_ use stays subsidised at 7–30x plan price for heavy users, and its allowance has net **expanded** over the last 12 months.

That is **reading (c), not (a) or (b)**:

- **(a) headline prices rise to API-equivalent: REFUTED.** 17 months flat at Anthropic; net decreases and three new tiers (two cheaper) at OpenAI; API prices falling toward the plans, not the reverse.
- **(b) allowance shrinks until value/price ≈ 1x: NOT SUPPORTED in general, TRUE in one place.** Measured 7.25x–36.8x for heavy Claude Code users; five-hour limits doubled permanently; +50% weekly extended five times; Codex limits doubled and extended to Free/Go. The single true instance is the frontier model on Pro/Team-Standard, where it is already 1.0x — and Max still gets Fable 5.1 at 50% of weekly limits seven days after launch.
- **(c) programmatic use metered at API list while interactive stays subsidised: SUPPORTED, and it is the only reading with a published instrument.**

**By when:** already in force for 3 of 4 channels (overflow, enterprise, third-party harnesses) and for the frontier model on Pro. For the ACP/`claude -p` channel: unresolved, **no announced date**, paused 85 days. Base case **within 12 months** (low 0 months — it can resume on notice at any time; high 24 months or never, if capacity keeps landing). The plan price stays $20/$100/$200 in every scenario found.

## Model consequence

**1. For TS-03 (ACP-driven Claude Code sessions), the convergence question is nearly moot — Twilight cannot legitimately run client work on a subscription regardless of how P1 resolves.**

- `code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance`: OAuth is "intended exclusively for purchasers... to support ordinary use of Claude Code"; "Advertised usage limits for Pro and Max plans assume **ordinary, individual usage**"; Anthropic "does not permit third-party developers to... route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials **on behalf of their users**." Agent SDK developers "should use API key authentication."
- Twilight's own A34 already assumes API-key shape: "repo/provider-scoped integration secrets... ephemeral read-only credential mount; never use a shared operator home," one credential identity per client repo × provider.
- `design.md:257-276` requires `billedCost` = "the provider's reported figure" reconciled against `estimatedCost` from a pinned rate card. **A subscription emits no per-run billedCost, so the ledger's reconciliation requirement is unsatisfiable on a plan.** The shipped design already presupposes metered billing.
- Even the paused carve-out would be _survivable, not fatal_: a $200 Max 20x credit at API list buys, at the balanced blend of $0.812/M processed, **$200 / $0.812 = 246M processed tokens/month = 24.6 p50 medium features**; at a Fable-5.1-heavy blend ($2.225/M) = 89.9M = **9.0 features**. Low/base/high: **9 / 17 / 25 p50 medium features per $200 seat-month.**

**2. The line item P1 is about is under 1% of revenue. This is the number that should resize the premise.**
Per accepted medium feature (tokens/accepted-unit p50 10M processed input / 150k output; p90 40M / 500k; formula `U·p_in·1.25 + C·p_cache + O·p_out` at h=0.90):

| Config                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | $/M processed | p50 feature (10M) | p90 feature (40M) |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ----------------- | ----------------- |
| all Sonnet 5                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | $0.580        | **$5.80**         | $22.20            |
| balanced blend (60/30/10)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | $0.812        | **$8.12**         | $31.60            |
| all Fable 5.1                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | $2.225        | **$22.25**        | $84.00            |
| Against the revenue band for a medium accepted feature ($1,500–$8,000): model cost = **0.07% – 5.6% of price**; base case $8.12/$3,500 = **0.23%**. At 20 accepted features/month the whole model bill is **$116 / $162 / $1,680 per month** = **$1,392 / $1,944 / $20,160 per year**. |

**3. Therefore P1 cannot carry P2.** The harness build is Tasks 1–16, **303–604 human hours** by line-item sum (M1 alone 111–220 h; personal loop 280–560 h and 3.5–9.2M tokens). At the derived $100–300/operator-hour that is **$30,300 – $181,200** of operator time. Ratio to the annual model bill P1 is about: **$30,300/$20,160 = 1.5x** (most favourable) to **$181,200/$1,392 = 130x**; base/base ≈ **$105,750 / $1,944 = 54x**. Build the harness because it is the product (accepted outcomes, evidence, budgets, critics/judge), **not** as a hedge against token pricing — the hedge is worth ≤$20k/yr against a ≥$30k build.

**4. Two shipped-config faults this analysis exposes, both worth a change.**

- **The balanced money cap is non-binding by ~30x against its own token cap.** For `money: $40` to bind before `tokens: 1,500,000`, the blended rate must exceed $40/1.5M = **$26.67 per 1M counted tokens**. Max achievable at list is Fable 5.1 100%-output at $50/M; at a realistic 1.5% output share the balanced blend is $0.812/M, i.e. **32.8x below the binding rate**. The `$40` cap will essentially never fire. (Thorough: $100/3M = $33.33/M — same conclusion. Economy: $12/500k = $24/M — same.)
- **Whether "tokens" includes cache reads decides whether balanced can finish a median feature at all.** A p50 medium feature is 10M _processed_ input. If the adapter counts cache reads, 10M > 1.5M → the run **pauses at 15% of a median feature** (6.7x over cap). If it counts only uncached input, that is 0.1 × 10M = 1.0M < 1.5M → it fits with 33% headroom. The brief's own note that this is "adapter-defined" is a **10–30x** ambiguity sitting on the primary control. Fix before M1 measures anything, or M1 measures the adapter, not the factory.

**5. Two second-order cost mechanisms Twilight has left unselected and should not.**

- **Cache TTL across the mandatory approval pause.** `approvals: [beforeStage.implementation]` is a human gate in the middle of every run. On a subscription the Claude Code cache TTL is 1 hour; on usage credits or an API key the default drops to **5 minutes**. Re-writing 200k of run context after a >5-min approval wait costs 0.2M × cache-write: Sonnet 5 **$0.50**, Opus 5 **$1.25**, Fable 5.1 **$2.50** — per pause, per deliverable, per rework round. At 12–25 attempts per balanced medium feature this is a real fraction of the $8.12 base.
