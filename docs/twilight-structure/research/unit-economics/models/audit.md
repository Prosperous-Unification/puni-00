# Arithmetic audit

## Overall

Fifteen non-reproducing figures across the three models; none overturns a headline. bottom-up-cost is the most internally sound — every one of its ten activity lines, all nine T1 price rows, both lognormal fits and all nine full-stack totals reproduce; its only real slip is an Opus token share of 42.3% that recomputes to 45.3%. top-down-business carries three stale headline fields whose own formula fields already contain the corrected values ($4,180 vs $3,885; $4,395 vs $4,424; $1,200 vs $3,699), an unreconcilable $41 low model cost, and one substantive error: MiniMax M3 and DeepSeek V4 Pro token multipliers used un-renormalised against Sonnet 5 (5.5x and 1.6x instead of 2.98x and 0.85x), which manufactures its "worse than Sonnet" conclusion for both. harness-payback-timeline has the largest single defect: its headline 650-950x token-estimate error rests on assuming agent-hours = 1.0-2.0x human hours while tasks.md states them explicitly (M1: 38-85 agent hours, not 151-600); using the plan's own figures the factor is 133-164x and M1 build model spend is $310-$745, not $1,300-$11,000. Its per-unit $44.20 also charges dev-sweeps at the $8/night cap against a $0.136/night computed spend and bakes in a x1.33 yield uplift, making its "harness is 1.5-3x more expensive" claim overstated at the top. The three models agree far more than they appear to: their effective token prices land within 12% of each other ($0.88-$1.00 per M processed incl. output), and essentially the entire spread in cost per accepted medium feature ($11.56 / $17.17 / $44.20) is one unmeasured quantity — the Twilight ceremony multiplier on the shared 10M/150k per-PR anchor (1.25x / 1.72x / 2.5x). Consensus carries the bottom-up build: $16 per accepted medium feature at S1, $3 small, $75 epic. Four conclusions survive all three independently and should be treated as settled: model tokens are 0.5-6% of unit cost and 0.2-1.1% of unit price at every operator rate and in every routing scenario; open-weight routing at the implement tier saves nothing because at h=0.90 the bill is cache reads and GLM-5.3's $0.26 read exceeds Sonnet 5's $0.20; the balanced 1,500,000-token cap is the highest-leverage undefined line in the schema, and at the consensus token volume it is breached at p50 under BOTH readings of "tokens" (uncached+output p50 = 1.73M), pausing 31-56% of medium features on the generous reading and 99.6% on the processed one; and the Max 20x subsidy cannot follow the business into client delivery, so it prices the build, not the product. Two inputs the models could not resolve are now answerable from research that landed after they ran: infra prices (Netcup EUR10.37-47.95, Browserbase $20/mo for 100 hrs at $0.12/hr overage) put shared infra at ~$100/month rather than the $220-500 assumed, and falsify bottom-up's $2.00-per-cloud-browser-session line by ~50x; and self-hosting is not economical below ~5-6 billion output tokens/month, which closes P3's strongest form. The three quantities a final report must flag as unmeasured rather than estimated are human minutes per accepted unit (80-96% of unit cost, invented in all three, and the repo forbids inferring it), the per-stage token distribution (inherited inference in all three), and the realised cache hit rate under per-activity model switching (unmodelled in all three, worth ~26% of every dollar figure).

## Cross-model disagreements (12)

- **Model cost per accepted MEDIUM feature, Anthropic list, h=0.90**: bottom-up $17.17 (17.23M processed / 299k out); top-down $11.56 (10M x F=1.25); payback $44.20 (25M, x1.33 unaccepted-run uplift, +$15 sweep)
  - cause: Not pricing — all three effective rates agree within 12% ($0.88-$1.00 per M processed incl. output). The whole spread is (a) the Twilight ceremony multiplier applied to the same 10M/150k per-PR p50 anchor: 1.25x vs 1.72x vs 2.5x, and (b) two uplifts only payback applies: x1.33 for unsuccessful runs and a dev-sweep line charged at the $8/night CAP instead of its $0.136/night computed spend. Payback's 2.5x rests on 'implementation is ~40% of the pipeline', which treats the 10M anchor as implementation-only; the anchor (Bun per-commit, merged-feature study) is already end-to-end including review cycles, so that is a double count.
  - resolution: Carry the bottom-up build. It is the only activity-level construction, every one of its ten activity lines reproduces to the cent, and its 1.72x implied ceremony multiplier sits between the other two. Consensus base $16 per accepted medium feature at S1 (excluding unaccepted-run uplift), band $8-$50. Apply unaccepted runs separately as a stated yield divisor, not baked in.
- **Processed input tokens per accepted medium feature**: bottom-up 17.23M; top-down 12.5M-equivalent (10M x F 1.25); payback 25M
  - cause: Same as above: the ceremony multiplier on the shared 10M p50 anchor. No measured per-stage distribution exists in the repo; all three inherit one researcher's explicitly-inferred stage table.
  - resolution: Consensus 17M base (band 10M-45M). Ceremony multiplier 1.25 / 1.7 / 2.5. This is the single input that most needs M1 ledgers.
- **Share of medium features that pause on the balanced 1,500,000-token cap**: bottom-up 63.5% (uncached+out, h=0.90) / 99.8% (processed); top-down 'p50 fits with 23% headroom' under uncached+out, 6.7x over under processed; payback 'the token limb is 30x tighter than the money limb, every real run pauses'
  - cause: Two compounding differences: the undefined 'tokens' semantic (adapter-defined per the schema) and the token-volume disagreement above. Top-down's 'fits with 23% headroom' is an artefact of its low 10M base: 1.0M uncached + 0.15M out = 1.15M. At the consensus 17M it does not fit.
  - resolution: At consensus p50 17M / p90 45M the cap is breached at p50 under BOTH readings: uncached+out p50 = 1.73M > 1.5M. Consensus pause share for a medium feature: 31% (h=0.95, uncached+out), 56% (h=0.90, uncached+out), 99.6% (processed). Money cap $40 pauses ~15%. The token limb fires first in every reading and is the highest-leverage undefined line in the schema.
- **Human minutes per accepted medium unit**: bottom-up 125 min (7 touchpoints incl. 30 min client comms and 25 min request shaping); top-down 90 min delivery touch + 4 h/client-month overhead (= ~170 min all-in at 3 units); payback 30 min steady-state with harness vs 180 without
  - cause: Scope, not measurement. Bottom-up puts client communication and request shaping inside the unit; top-down puts client overhead outside the unit and then re-adds it per client-month; payback counts only the mandated approval plus pause handling at steady state and books everything else as sales. No measured figure exists anywhere in the repo, and design.md forbids inferring it from approval waiting.
  - resolution: Split the quantity. Delivery touch per medium unit: 30 / 75 / 150 min. Client overhead: 2 / 4 / 8 h per client-month. All-in per medium unit at 3 units/client-month: 45 / 120 / 240 min. Payback's 30 min is a steady-state floor, not a planning figure; bottom-up's 125 is the right planning number and lands inside the consensus band.
- **Infrastructure cost per accepted medium unit and shared infra per month**: bottom-up $6.97/unit (of which Browserbase $2.40 at an assumed $2.00/session) on a $150/$350/$700-per-month pool; top-down $500/mo shared + $40/client-month (= ~$55/unit fully allocated) with Browserbase $50-200/mo; payback $60/$220/$550 per month during build, Browserbase $0/$99/$250
  - cause: All three declared infra unsourced. The infra sheet now supplies real prices and falsifies the largest single line: Browserbase is $20/mo for 100 hours with $0.12/hr overage (Startup $99/mo for 500 hrs), i.e. a cloud-browser acceptance pass costs cents, not $2.00. Netcup VPS is confirmed at EUR10.37 (4/8), EUR19.25 (8/16), EUR47.95 (16/64); Grafana Cloud free tier covers observability; Backblaze B2 $6.95/TB-mo.
  - resolution: Shared infra: 1 server + 2 agent nodes + h3mon + h4claw on Netcup = EUR69.61 (~$75) + Browserbase $20 + storage/registry ~$5 = ~$100/mo base ($60 low, $250 high with 16/64 nodes and Browserbase Startup). Per accepted unit, fully allocated at 12 units/month: $2 / $8 / $25. Marginal per-unit infra is under $0.50. Bottom-up's $6.97 lands near the consensus by coincidence — its per-unit rate is ~4-7x too high and its Browserbase line ~50x too high, offsetting.
- **Revenue per accepted unit and per client-month**: bottom-up medium $1,500-8,000 (the research band verbatim); top-down $1,250-1,500 blended per unit implied by a $4,500 retainer at 3 units; payback $800 / $2,000 / $4,500
  - cause: Bottom-up quotes the research band's full width without picking; top-down back-solves a per-unit price from a chosen retainer and a chosen cadence (both free variables); payback weights toward the floor (Fraction $99/point -> $792-1,485 for an 8-15 point feature) on the reasoning that a solo operator selling to SMBs lands in the lower half.
  - resolution: Carry payback's weighting, widened at the top: medium $800 / $2,000 / $5,000; small $300 / $700 / $2,000; epic $4,000 / $9,000 / $20,000. Retainer $2,000 / $4,500 / $8,000 per client-month, valid only if the unit cadence is published in the retainer (top-down's own point: a $4,500 retainer at 6 units implies $750/unit, below the small band's floor).
- **Harness build cost (revenue-relevant personal loop)**: bottom-up: not computed; top-down $21,000-$42,000 human at $75/h plus $3-$85 of tokens; payback $16,682 / $72,796 / $195,060 all-in at $50/$150/$300 per hour
  - cause: Hour base agrees (tasks.md line 27 states 280-560 h for the personal loop, confirmed). The spread is (a) the operator rate — $75 vs $50/$150/$300 — and (b) the build token line: top-down takes the plan's 3.5-9.2M at face value ($3-$85); payback restates it to $2,400-$20,400 via an assumed 1.0-2.0 agent-h per human-h. tasks.md gives agent hours explicitly and they imply ~0.25-0.28 agent-h per human-h.
  - resolution: Human hours 280 / 420 / 560 (tasks.md, confirmed). Build model spend $600 / $2,500 / $11,000 — the low end is the plan's own agent-hour ratio at 9.4M processed/agent-h, the high end is a genuinely agent-led build at 1.5x. Infra during build $150 / $700 / $1,500. All-in: $16,700 at $50/h, $34,700 at $75/h, $66,400 at $150/h, $175,000 at $300/h. Both the top-down token figure (implausibly low) and the payback token figure (~5x overstated) should be dropped.
- **Binding throughput ceiling**: bottom-up: human minutes, at every rate and scenario; top-down: demand first, then K3s pool at ~3 concurrent clients, then human minutes at 8-19 clients; payback: serialised staging (one environment, buildSlots 1, browserSlots 1, immediate-never-coalesce)
  - cause: Three different horizons. Payback names staging without computing its capacity; top-down computes it (~6-13 medium features per client-DAY on serialised resources) against a demand of 3-4.5 units per client-MONTH, which makes staging a burst constraint by a factor of ~60. Bottom-up never models capacity at all.
  - resolution: Order: (1) demand/sales, binding today at 2-6 clients; (2) the balanced 1.5M-token cap, which under the consensus token volume pauses 31-56% of medium features even on the generous semantic and converts token risk into operator interrupts — the binding operational ceiling until the adapter contract defines 'tokens'; (3) human minutes at 8-19 clients / 24-57 accepted units per month; (4) the K3s pool at ~3 concurrent clients, relievable at EUR19.25 per added node; (5) serialised staging/build/browser slots — bursts only. Payback's staging claim is not supported by its own arithmetic.
- **Whether the harness raises or lowers cost per accepted unit**: bottom-up: Twilight costs 1.7-2.2x a plain harness, and that is a defensible price; top-down: does not compare; payback: the harness makes each unit 1.5-3x MORE expensive ($44 vs $22) and anyone selling it on token savings is selling the wrong thing
  - cause: Same underlying claim; payback's 3x upper end is inflated by the two errors above (sweep at cap, x1.33 baked in) and by comparing an API-list harness cost against a marginal-zero subscription baseline.
  - resolution: Consensus: the harness raises model cost per accepted unit by 1.5-2.0x ($16 vs ~$9-11 for a plain Claude-Code-class run on the same feature) and lowers operator minutes. Both directions are real; the model-cost increase is 1-2% of the unit price and the operator-minute reduction is 80-95% of it.
- **Value of routing to open-weight models (P3)**: bottom-up: implement-only swap to GLM-5.3 saves $0.03/unit (0.2%); all-GLM $10.21 vs $17.17; cheapest viable is DeepSeek at $5.15 but inadmissible; top-down: GLM implement-only is 1.01x (no saving) and M3/DeepSeek are worse than Sonnet on an effective basis; payback: aggressive all-GLM saves $8.70/unit (~20-30%)
  - cause: All three agree on the mechanism (at h=0.90 cache reads dominate and GLM-5.3's $0.26 cache read exceeds Sonnet 5's $0.20). The residual spread is the attempts basis: payback gives GLM 1.0 attempts against Sonnet's 1.75 (inconsistent with the pass@1 figures it cites), and top-down's 'M3/DeepSeek worse than Sonnet' rests on un-renormalised token multipliers.
  - resolution: Implement-only GLM swap: 1.00x, no saving — all three agree, carry it. All-classes GLM: 0.65-0.70x on the model line ($11 vs $16), i.e. ~$5/unit, ~0.25% of a $2,000 unit price. DeepSeek V4 Pro and MiniMax M3 are inadmissible under rework.maxRounds 2 regardless of price. The largest single routing lever in all three models is a closed model (GPT-5.6 Sol, 0.13x Sonnet-relative token volume, ~$3/unit) resting on one SWE-rebench row at promotional pricing to 2026-11-21.
- **Value of the Max 20x subscription subsidy**: bottom-up: worth 7.2x-29x on model tokens, 3.0-5.1% of full unit cost, 'a 5% discount with 100% policy risk'; top-down: a $200 seat is a NET LOSS of $89/month at base volumes and A34 makes it structurally impossible; payback: $1,510-$23,660 of captured BUILD subsidy, worth 5-15% of build cost, and pace beats subsidy 19-76:1
  - cause: Different denominators, not different facts. All three use the same sourced anchors ($200 seat; $150-250/dev-mo Anthropic enterprise; viberank median $1,450, top decile $7,358, Aug-2026 monthly median $6,085; community heavy-Opus ~$5,800). Bottom-up measures the subsidy against per-unit model spend; top-down against the factory's total API-list demand; payback against the build.
  - resolution: Absorbable ceiling per seat: $200 (low, Anthropic's own enterprise figure) / $1,450 (base, viberank calculator median) / $5,800-6,085 (high). But the factory's own API-list model demand at base cadence is $110-$250/month, so a delivery-only seat is at or below break-even; the seat pays for itself only on interactive and build use. All three agree ToS forbids routing Pro/Max credentials on behalf of clients, and A34's per-repo ephemeral credential mounts are incompatible with one person's OAuth token. Carry: one seat for interactive/build work, API keys for every worker attempt.
- **Self-hosted open-weight inference**: all three: PENDING, no self-hosting sheet was present
  - cause: The self-hosting sheet was written after the three models ran.
  - resolution: Now answerable and it closes P3's strongest form: at 200M-1B tokens/month with 90% cache reads, an 8-GPU H100/H200 node runs under 3% utilisation, pushing effective self-hosted cost into the tens of dollars per MTok. Break-even against the cheapest comparable hosted price ($3.96-4.40/MTok out) is ~5-6 BILLION output tokens/month — 5-30x this factory's volume. Self-hosting is not economical for a solo operator; remove it from the decision.

## Unsupported assumptions

- Per-stage token distribution. All three models inherit one researcher's explicitly self-labelled INFERENCE (research-cost-structure fact 70) for the implementation/review/discovery/specification/planning/knowledge/integration split. No measured per-stage distribution exists in the repo and tasks.md forbids setting a default profile or savings target before M1 ledgers. Every per-unit dollar figure in all three models rests on it.
- Cache hit rate h=0.90 applied uniformly to every activity class. Observed rates (94.8% Anthropic session example, 92.4% Bun, 91% /usage, 97% viberank Aug-2026) are all from interactive single-model sessions. Twilight switches model per activity class (sonnet implement -> opus review -> haiku knowledge), and every switch invalidates the prefix; prompt-cache TTL and compaction are deliberately unselected (grill Q30/Q81/Q85), and the TTL falls from 1 hour to 5 minutes once drawing on usage credits. None of the three models the per-class cache reset. This is the largest unmodelled mechanism and it moves every dollar figure ~26% between h=0.85 and h=0.95, more if the real rate is far lower.
- Human minutes per touchpoint. Invented in all three (bottom-up's 7-touchpoint table, top-down's 0.4/1.5/5.0 h, payback's 45/30/24 min). design.md:650-651 requires human effort be explicitly measured and never inferred from approval waiting; no measured figure exists anywhere in the repo. This is 80-96% of unit cost.
- Browserbase at $0.50/$2.00/$5.00 per acceptance session (bottom-up A11), $50-200/month (top-down), $0/$99/$250 per month (payback). Falsified by the infra sheet: Developer $20/mo includes 100 hours with $0.12/hr overage, Startup $99/mo includes 500 hours at $0.10/hr. A cloud-browser acceptance pass costs cents. bottom-up's Browserbase line is 34% of its infra total at a rate ~50x the metered price.
- All K3s pool prices. bottom-up A10 ($150/$350/$700 per month), top-down (K3s server $24-48, agent nodes $48-96 each, $500 shared base), payback ($60/$220/$550 per month) are each declared unsourced. Netcup confirms EUR10.37 / EUR19.25 / EUR47.95 for the 4/8, 8/16 and 16/64 SKUs; Hetzner's CX/CPX/CCX/AX prices were NOT retrievable (JS-hydrated). The consensus pool is ~$75/month, 2-9x below the assumed figures.
- Pool utilisation. bottom-up's infra range spans 68x ($1.14 to $77.91 per medium feature) entirely on an unsourced 10-50% utilisation assumption; its 'high' band multiplies high agent-hours by the low-utilisation rate, which the model itself labels an incoherent corner.
- Per-attempt Job size of 2 vCPU / 4 GiB on 8 vCPU / 16 GiB nodes with 3 concurrent attempts per node. design.md:378-389 states the CPU/memory/ephemeral-storage/deadline bounds exist but publishes no values. The entire ~3-concurrent-client capacity ceiling rests on it.
- Agent-hours per human hour for the build. payback assumes 1.0-2.0x and explicitly treats tasks.md's own agent-hour line items as an error. tasks.md states them per task (M1: 38-85 agent hours against 151-300 human hours, i.e. ~0.25-0.28x). Whether the plan models a human-led build or has a unit error is unresolved, and the two readings differ by ~5x on build model spend.
- Transfer of SWE-rebench token-volume multipliers, measured on an implement-shaped benchmark task with a fixed ReAct scaffold, to plan, review, judge, knowledge and cloud-browser classes. bottom-up quantifies the risk (Fable-everywhere moves $19.17 -> $34.23 if the 0.56x does not transfer) and flags it as its shakiest assumption; the other two apply it silently.
- Attempts multipliers for Haiku 4.5 (1.5x/3.0x) and GLM-5.3-Flash (2.0x/2.5x). Neither appears in SWE-rebench or any other source. The economy profile's entire cost case rests on the Haiku figure, and the two models that use it reach opposite conclusions (0.54x vs a false economy).
- The x1.33 unsuccessful-run uplift (payback) and the 0.55-0.95 acceptance-yield band (bottom-up). design.md:745-748 requires unsuccessful runs in the numerator, but nothing in the repo estimates the yield, and the 30-day escaped-defect window means even accepted outcomes are provisional.
- Lognormal fitting of per-unit cost and token distributions to p50/p90 (bottom-up A16, sigma = ln(p90/p50)/1.2816). A convention, not an observed distribution; every pause-share percentage inherits it.
- The 15% price premium for staging-proven acceptance (payback). No source prices acceptance evidence directly; the supporting evidence is indirect (Futurum 27% outcome-pricing preference, Productive.io 52% held-or-raised price, AsyncForge's turnaround-based tiers).
- With-harness and without-harness monthly revenue ceilings ($18k -> $34k at base, payback). No evidence in any sheet. This single pair of numbers produces the entire base payback of 4.8 months; on the model's own no-demand-capture floor the answer is 8.9 months and in the worst corner 51.9.
- Retainer price ($4,500) and unit cadence (3 accepted units per client-month) in top-down. Both are free variables chosen so the implied blended $/unit stays inside the derived bands; the scenario is internally consistent, not observed.
- Charging dev-sweeps at the $8/night money CAP rather than the ~$0.136/night computed spend (payback), and 25 sweep-nights per client-month (top-down). The cap is ~59x expected spend; whether an onTrigger retry (max 2) opens a fresh per-occurrence account is unstated in the schema.
- Agent-time conversion: 8 minutes per 1M processed (bottom-up A9) vs 9.4M processed per agent-hour = 6.4 min per 1M (payback). Both derive from the same single Anthropic session example (991k processed in 6m20s of API duration); they differ by 18% and drive both the agentTime cap analysis and the whole build-token restatement.
- The escalated-attempt token premium of 1.3x and the 0.00/0.25/1.00 escalated-attempts-per-deliverable split (bottom-up A6). Unsourced, and escalation is 18.4% of that model's medium-feature model cost.
- The convergence-date band for the paused Agent SDK / claude -p / ACP carve-out (2026-10-01 / 2027-01-15 / 2027-07-01, payback). No date has been announced; Anthropic promised advance notice. It moves the build-subsidy figure by an order of magnitude at the high end.
- Self-hosted inference declared PENDING by all three. The self-hosting sheet now resolves it against them: at 200M-1B tokens/month with 90% cache reads a large-MoE 8-GPU deployment runs under 3% utilisation and break-even against hosted pricing is ~5-6 billion output tokens per month. No model should carry self-hosting as an open lever.
