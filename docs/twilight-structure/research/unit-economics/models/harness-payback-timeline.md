# Investment-analyst read on the Twilight harness as a capital project and on its timing against the paused subscription/API convergence. Three things dominate every other number. (1) The plan's token line items are not consumption estimates — restated bottom-up from three independent anchors they are ~650–950x low, which means the build's model spend is $1k–$12k (small, not free) and, far more importantly, that the same unit error sits inside the shipped run budgets: the token limb of every profile is 20–60x tighter than its own money and agentTime limbs and will pause real runs first. (2) The harness does NOT lower cost per accepted unit — at Anthropic list it raises it ~1.5–3x over Dany driving Claude Code, because it adds discovery, spec critique, a critic, a judge, knowledge reconciliation and a browser acceptance pass. Its value is operator-hour leverage, a sellable acceptance warranty, and a ledger; token savings are a rounding error. (3) The timing race against P1 is largely a false objective: Anthropic's own legal page already forbids routing Pro/Max credentials on behalf of users, so for client-serving delivery P1 is TRUE TODAY. Winning the race buys subsidized BUILD tokens worth $1.5k–$24k, while landing the revenue-relevant scope one month earlier is worth ~$15k. Pace should be set by revenue-start date, not by subsidy capture. All figures are low/base/high with arithmetic shown; self-hosting economics are PENDING (no self-hosting research sheet was present in the research directory).

## Premise implications

P1 (subscriptions converge to API prices) is not a future event for this business — it is already true for the part that matters. Anthropic's Claude Code legal page forbids routing Pro/Max credentials "on behalf of their users", and Enterprise is already "$20/seat + usage at API rates", so every token Twilight spends on a client repository is an API-list token from the first paying client onward, whatever happens to the paused carve-out. What P1's timing actually decides is the price of the BUILD, worth $1,510–$23,660 of captured subsidy, against a build cost of $16,682–$195,060 and a delivery value of $3,760–$28,000 per month. That is a 5–15% line item, and one month of earlier delivery outweighs it 19–76×. The correct response to P1 is therefore not to race: it is (a) build interactively on the subscription while that is permitted and defer Task 14's self-growing-repo dogfooding, which converts subsidised tokens into metered ones at a 7–17× cash multiple; (b) stop treating the subsidy as a strategic asset, because it cannot legally follow the business into revenue.

P2 (a great custom harness that can use cheaper models) survives, but not for the reason stated. The harness does not make delivery cheaper — at Anthropic list it makes each accepted unit 1.5–3× MORE expensive ($44 vs $22 at base), because it adds discovery, spec critique, a critic, a judge, knowledge reconciliation and a browser acceptance pass that Dany does not pay for when driving Claude Code himself. What it buys is a 5–10× reduction in operator minutes per accepted unit (180 → 30), an independent acceptance oracle that is mandatory and cannot be disabled, and a ledger that measures cost per accepted outcome. Those are throughput and accountability, and they are what the payback rests on: 4.4–7.0 months matched, 8.9 months if the freed hours capture no additional demand, 51.9 months in the worst corner. The single largest risk to P2 is not technical — it is that the supply ceiling rises and the binding constraint moves to sales, where a solo operator has no leverage from the harness at all.

P3 (open weights ok and much cheaper) is the premise the numbers damage most. At the 90% cache hit rates this workload actually runs at, cost is dominated by cache reads, and GLM-5.3's cache read ($0.26/MTok) is ABOVE Sonnet 5's ($0.20). The 4.65× sticker advantage over Opus 5 collapses to an 18% effective advantage over Sonnet 5, and the aggressive all-GLM routing saves $8.70 out of $44.20 per unit — about $390/month, $9,400 over 24 months, which is 5–13% of the personal-loop build cost. Models that are genuinely cheap per token (DeepSeek V4 Pro at $0.26/MTok effective) carry a ~5× attempts multiplier that eats the entire advantage; MiniMax M3 carries a 5.5× token multiplier. Cheap open weights need the verifier the harness provides, and once you pay for the verifier the residual saving is small. Meanwhile the cheapest credible route in the whole table is a CLOSED model — GPT-5.6 Sol at medium effort, 0.24× token volume, $1.81 per implementation unit — and Sonnet 5's cancelled price increase did more for these unit economics than the entire open-weight tier. P3 should be reframed: the harness's model-routing value is not "open weights are cheap", it is "a verifier lets me shop the whole frontier, including whichever closed mid-tier is currently on promotion", and that reframing survives P3 being false.

## Findings

- The plan's token estimates are ~650–950× low as consumption figures, and ~65–95× low even under the most charitable reading (tokens = uncached + output at h=0.90). Three independent estimators agree within a factor of 3 that M1 consumes 1.4–5.6 BILLION processed tokens, not 2.18–6.02 million. Restated M1 model spend is $1,300 / $4,500 / $11,000 — small in absolute terms, which is why the error has gone unnoticed, but the same unit is carried into the run budgets where it is load-bearing.
- The material consequence: the token limb of every shipped budget vector is 21–62× tighter than the money and agentTime limbs of the SAME vector. Balanced's $40 buys 45.5M processed tokens and its PT6H buys ~56M, but its token cap is 1,500,000. Money and agentTime triangulate; tokens is the outlier under both cache-accounting readings. Every real feature run will pause on the token limb long before it approaches its money or time budget. Fix: derive tokens = money ÷ pinned blended effective rate, or delete the limb.
- The harness raises cost per accepted unit by 1.5–3× ($44.20 vs $22 at base) and the whole of that increase is cash at API list, while the interactive baseline is ~$0 marginal on a Max 20x plan today. Anyone selling this internally on token savings is selling the wrong thing. The defensible case is 180 → 30 operator-minutes per accepted unit, a 30%→12% post-delivery rework rate, and a measured cost-per-accepted-outcome the client can be shown.
- At h=0.90 the open-weight price advantage nearly vanishes: GLM-5.3's cache read ($0.26/MTok) is ABOVE Sonnet 5's ($0.20), so GLM-5.3 is only 18% cheaper than Sonnet 5 in effective terms despite a 4.65× sticker advantage over Opus 5. Aggressive all-GLM routing saves $8.70/unit (~20%), about $390/month at base volume — 5–13% of the personal-loop build cost. P3, routed through this harness's own profile shape, does not pay for the harness.
- The cheapest credible implementation route in the entire comparison is closed, not open: GPT-5.6 Sol at medium effort, 0.24× token volume (SWE-rebench, same scaffold), $1.81 per accepted implementation unit vs Sonnet 5's $10.15 — 5.6× cheaper than the shipped balanced default. It is on promotional pricing to 2026-11-21. Every shipped profile in execution.yaml is Anthropic-only (every `provider:` key in all three profiles and both escalation ladders); the plan has no mechanism to notice or exploit this.
- DeepSeek V4 Pro's ~5× attempts multiplier (pass@1 40.2 vs pass@5 64.0) consumes its entire price advantage: $11.22 per accepted unit vs Sonnet 5's $10.15, despite being 2.2× cheaper per token. MiniMax M3's 5.5× token multiplier does the same. The verifier makes cheap models POSSIBLE, not cheap — which is exactly the claim P2 should make and P3 should not.
- The balanced escalation ladder is the one shipped design choice the arithmetic positively validates. Sonnet-5-then-Opus-5-high, both attempts charged, beats going Opus-first as long as Sonnet fails less than 57.0% of the time; SWE-rebench puts Sonnet 5's pass@1 at 56.8%, i.e. a 43.2% failure rate, a 13.8-point margin. Keep it.
- For the client-serving business, P1 is already true. Anthropic's legal page forbids routing Pro/Max credentials 'on behalf of their users' and Enterprise is already '$20/seat + usage at API rates'. The paused carve-out therefore only decides the price of the BUILD, not of delivery. Racing it is optimising a 5–15% line item.
- Pace beats subsidy 19–76 to 1. Total build subsidy is nearly invariant to how fast Dany works (it is per-token-consumed, not per-month; 60→140 h/mo captures only ~$1,000 more, in avoided plan fees), while moving the personal loop 1.67 months earlier is worth $25,290 at base ΔNet. Choose pace on revenue-start date.
- Dogfooding the harness on its own build converts subsidised tokens into metered tokens at a 7–17× cash multiple ($1,450/mo API-equivalent vs $200 paid, viberank median). Task 14's self-growing-repo work should be sequenced after the carve-out lands or after client revenue starts, not before.
- M1 cannot generate revenue. factory-core terminates at handoff with staging.deploy, acceptance.cloud-browser, publication.main and release.production all disabled; the staging-proven acceptance that the entire pricing premium rests on arrives only with Task 13, in M4. The correct capital-budgeting unit is the plan's own 'personal loop' (280–560 h, $16,682 / $72,796 / $195,060), not M1 and not M1–M4.
- Throughput with the harness is bounded by staging, not by tokens or by human minutes. Staging is `immediate-never-coalesce` with one environment, one build slot and one browser slot, and a moved main restarts composition, build, staging and acceptance. At 24+ units/month that serialised path, plus one agent-driven cloud-browser pass per staging candidate, is the queue that decides monthly volume. It is the first thing to instrument in M1 and it is not in the scaling-acceptance matrix (which fixes fanOut/perClientCeiling/agentSlots but not staging concurrency).
- Payback on the personal loop is 4.4 / 4.8 / 7.0 months matched, but the range across crossed corners is 0.6 to 51.9 months, and the entire spread is driven by one assumption: whether freed operator hours convert into sold units. With zero demand capture the payback is 8.9 months on premium + freed-hour opportunity + rework alone, which is still defensible — so the project clears on its floor case, and the upside is a sales question, not an engineering one.
