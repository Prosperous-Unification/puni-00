What one accepted feature costs, and what the harness is actually for

A solo factory selling staging-proven, accepted software outcomes to SMB clients. Every number below is live: move the sliders to test the three premises against each other.

Model tokens are 1–3% of the price of an accepted outcome. A medium feature costs about $20 in tokens on Anthropic list prices through the full Twilight stage structure, against a market price of $1,500–8,000. Doubling the token price, or halving it with open weights, moves gross margin by about one percentage point.

The subsidy premise 1 expects to end is already gone for the factory's usage mode. Third-party harnesses were metered at API rates on 2026-04-04, and the carve-out for claude -p, the Agent SDK and ACP is drafted, paused since 2026-06-15. Plan on API list. At 20 units a month the subsidy would be worth about $10 per feature anyway.

What binds is human minutes. One operator has roughly 140 productive hours a month. The harness raises model cost per unit 1.5–2× over driving Claude Code by hand, because it adds discovery, critique, a judge and an acceptance pass. It earns its 300–700 build hours by cutting operator minutes per accepted outcome from about three hours to under one, raising the acceptance rate, and producing evidence a client will pay for. Its verifier is also what makes a cheaper implement model safe, which matters for the tail: epics, fan-out, nightly sweeps.

Three premises, tested against the evidence
Premise 1
Subscriptions will soon be priced close to API usage.
Holds, narrower
True at the margin and for programmatic use: every overflow token, every Enterprise token and every third-party harness token is already billed at API list, and OpenAI's Codex credits map to API list at exactly $0.04. Headline plan prices have not moved in 17 months; API prices fell toward them. Interactive use stays subsidised at 7–30× for heavy users, and a week after Fable 5.1 launched Max still gets it at half its weekly limit. For a factory that drives sessions over ACP the question is already settled by policy: plan credentials may not be used on behalf of clients.
Decisive signals: 2026-09-13, when the +50% weekly limits are due to revert; the pause banner on the Agent SDK plan article gaining an effective date; whether the next flagship launches credits-only on Max.
Premise 2
Until then, a custom harness that can use cheaper models is buildable.
Wrong as stated; build it anyway
There is no window: Twilight is API-metered by design (a rate card, provider-billed cost reconciled per attempt, per-repo credentials), so the subsidy it would race is one it cannot use. The build is 343–684 hours with allowances, four to nine months solo. And "cheaper models" is a config line, not a harness property: under the same scaffold every cheaper implement model costs as much as or more than the Sonnet 5 already shipped. What survives is worth more than the claim: operator minutes per accepted outcome cut roughly six-fold, an independent acceptance oracle, and the first honest measurement of cost per accepted feature.
Decisive signal: the first ten accepted-outcome ledgers, split model / tool / human minutes. If model tokens are under 10%, the cheaper-model half is closed on arithmetic.
Premise 3
Open-weight models will be OK for coding and much cheaper.
Holds, for named models
"OK" holds: GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek V4 Pro sit within a point of Opus 5 on an independent SWE-bench harness. "Much cheaper" does not. At a 90% cache hit rate the bill is cache reads, and GLM-5.3's ($0.26) is above Sonnet 5's ($0.20) and Fable 5.1's ($0.25); GLM's 2× edge per accepted outcome comes from pass@1 quality and inverts above a 93% hit rate. DeepSeek needs ~5 attempts, MiniMax burns 5.5× tokens, Kimi K3 is priced above Sonnet. The cheapest model per accepted feature in every estimate is closed: GPT-5.6 Sol at 0.24× the tokens.
Decisive signal: measured on Twilight's own fixture, GLM-5.3's cache hit rate, token multiplier and attempts-to-accept. Adopt only if h < 93%, tokens < 2.2× and attempts ≤ 1.2. No same-scaffold row exists yet for any 2026 open flagship.

The harness as an investment

Without the harness the counterfactual is the operator driving Claude Code interactively, one thing at a time, with no independent acceptance: more human minutes per outcome, fewer clients, and a price that is harder to defend because nothing but the operator's word proves the deliverable. With it, the operator approves a plan once, gets paused only when a run leaves its envelope, and sells acceptance evidence.

Personal delivery loop 280–560 h by the plan; all 16 tasks 343–684 h. The plan's M1 estimate grew 1.5× in one review, so add drift.
Interactive driving, manual review, manual UAT.

The plan that falls out of the numbers

  1. Price the model line at API list from day one. Treat any subscription-included usage as a windfall for the operator's own interactive work, never a plan input. Twilight already presupposes metered billing, needs a provider-billed cost per attempt to reconcile its ledger, and requires per-repo credentials that a personal seat cannot supply.
  2. Settle what a "token" is in the budget caps, then re-derive them from the first ledgers. Balanced's 1.5M-token limb is about 30× tighter than what its own $40 buys and 40× tighter than its six agent-hours; the money and time limbs agree with each other and the token limb is the outlier. At the consensus 17M processed tokens per medium feature it is breached at the median under both readings: nearly every feature pauses under processed semantics, 30–55% under uncached-plus-output, against about 15% for the $40 cap. Derive tokens as money divided by the pinned blended rate, or drop the limb.
  3. Measure human minutes per touchpoint from the first run. They are 80–96% of the cost of an accepted outcome and the one line the plan measures least precisely. Every pause the caps cause is roughly $50 of the expensive resource.
  4. Build the acceptance oracle and the ledger before anything that saves tokens. The oracle turns a cheaper model's pass@5 into an accepted outcome and is what lets you sell acceptance. The ledger produces the per-stage token distribution nobody has measured, and the plan's own token estimates are two orders of magnitude below consumption, so nothing downstream of them can be trusted yet.
  5. Sweep effort before switching model; the next adapter is Codex, not an open-weight one. Artificial Analysis measures Fable 5.1 at high effort costing half of max for two points, and GPT-6 Astra likewise; effort is the cheap lever and keeps one cache namespace. Then route by class: a flagship on plan, critic and judge, where one attempt has to be right. GPT-5.6 Sol on implement saves about 1.45× more per feature than GLM-5.3 for the same adapter cost and is already the plan's second probe. Batch the asynchronous review stages at 50% off; that alone is worth more than the whole open-weight substitution. Keep the Sonnet→Opus escalation ladder: it beats Opus-first while Sonnet fails under 57% of attempts.
  6. If an open-weight implement class is added, verify it with tools, not a flagship judge, and give it its own escalation target. A model that needs four attempts under an Opus judge is the worst of both. GLM-5.3 is the candidate; adopt it only when Twilight's own fixture measures a cache hit rate under 93%, a token multiplier under 2.2 and attempts to accept at or below 1.2. Never route MiniMax M3 to agentic coding, and use DeepSeek V4 Pro off-peak only for bulk, tool-verified, latency-tolerant work.
  7. Do not self-host. An 8×H200 node rents for about $32 an hour and decodes a DeepSeek-class model at roughly 2,200 tokens a second per GPU, which is $0.50 per million output tokens at full utilisation and break-even against hosted GLM or DeepSeek prices at 5–6 billion output tokens a month. A solo factory processes 0.2–1 billion tokens a month, 90% of them cache reads, so the node would sit below 3% utilisation and cost tens of dollars per million. Rent per token, prefer hosts that price cache reads.
  8. Build interactively on the subscription; do not run the build through the harness until revenue starts. Dogfooding converts subsidised tokens into metered ones at a 7–17× cash multiple. The subsidy is legitimate for one operator's own work and worth $1.5–24k over the build; one month of earlier revenue is worth more than the whole of it.
  9. Sell turnaround and acceptance, not hours. Price per unit is the lever worth ±50% of margin; every model lever together is worth about one point. The productized shops price by queue speed (€2k / €4k / €8k for 4 / 2 / 1 business days). Staging-proven acceptance justifies the upper half of each band. A retainer of $3–8k per client-month with a named unit allowance is the simplest contract; an unbounded allowance is the one shape where model cost could stop being noise.
  10. Protect the human bottleneck, and relieve the cheap one. Ceilings bind in this order: demand, then the token cap's pauses, then operator minutes at 8–19 clients, then the two-node pool at about three concurrent clients (€20–50 a month per extra node), and only in bursts the serialized staging path. Every design decision that removes a pause, an approval or a manual acceptance step raises revenue more than any model swap.
  11. Watch four dated signals. 2026-09-13, when the +50% Claude Code weekly limits are due to revert or be extended a sixth time. The pause banner on Anthropic's Agent SDK plan article gaining an effective date, which meters ACP at API list. 2026-11-21, when GPT-5.6 Sol's promo price reverts or is made permanent. And the day-one plan status of the next flagship on Max.

Self-hosting, and why it is out
  • On-demand GPU rental 2026-09-08: H100 $2.69–3.99 per GPU-hour on Lambda, RunPod, Nebius, Hyperstack, Verda; H200 $3.59–4.59; B200 $5.98–7.15; CoreWeave 40–70% above those; spot 40–60% below. Fireworks dedicated H100 $7–8, Together $3.99.
  • Throughput: DeepSeek-class MoE at 2,200 output tokens per second per H200 in vLLM's production wide-EP serving (Dec 2025); 2,785 per H100 in SGLang's large-scale EP run (May 2025); 10,100 per GB200 with NVFP4 (Feb 2026).
  • Derived: 8×H200 at $4 = $32/h → 17,600 tok/s → 63M tokens/h → $0.51 per million at 100% utilisation, $1.70 at 30%, $17 at 3%. Break-even against $3.96–4.40 hosted output needs ≥13% utilisation, about 5–6B output tokens a month, before any ops time. Prefix-cache hits are near-free compute, so the binding constraint at 100k+ contexts is KV-cache memory, not FLOPs.
  • Small models on 1–2 GPUs are not a route for accepted outcomes yet: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B resolves 24.7% of fresh issues (pass@5 43.2%).
