# PUNI acquisition and conversion experiments

Draft for discussion, 2026-10-24. This plan brings online traffic into the [landing prompt → chat → project request and email funnel](conversion-funnel-options.md). It proposes campaigns and content; it does not authorize ad spend, publishing, or contacting anyone.

**Recommendation:** keep the homepage broad enough to route the services, but launch one specific campaign and one evidence-based content series. Start with existing AI-built apps as a testable audience hypothesis. Combine focused search advertising, if relevant demand exists at viable prices, with public demonstrations and useful articles distributed where those buyers participate. Optimize for useful requests, then check whether those requests become conversations and paid work.

Selecting a first campaign does not remove the other services. It gives each ad or article a concrete reader and next step. No search volume, CPC, close rate, or demand advantage for these segments has been established in this research.

## A concrete search campaign hypothesis

Working audience: someone has built an app with AI, has a real next milestone, and wants engineering help. Pick one initial language and geography that PUNI can serve; those remain commercial decisions, not assumptions to hide in forecasts.

Candidate search themes, **not researched-volume keywords**:

- Help fixing an AI-built app.
- Developer to finish an app prototype.
- Help launching an existing web app.
- App keeps breaking after changes.

Start with commercial-intent variants. Tutorial and tool-shopping searches may be better served by articles. Review actual query relevance; likely exclusions include jobs, courses, and unrelated tools. Do not blanket-exclude a term such as “help” if it is central to the buyer's intent.

Two message concepts to test, not finalized ad-format assets:

**Problem-led:** “AI-built app stuck? Get help moving it forward.” Description: “Tell us what is breaking or unfinished. Clarify the next step and request a human review.”

**Outcome-led:** “Get your app ready for its next customers.” Description: “A focused starting point for your next release. Describe the goal or choose a few answers.”

Both lead to the same relevant version of the landing prompt. Do not advertise a free audit, instant quote, or guaranteed delivery unless that is a real, defined service.

Before spending, use the ad account's keyword planning estimates for the chosen location and language, check the resulting searches, and select a total experiment budget. Google explains that forecasts depend on settings and historical evidence and can differ from actual traffic. No account-specific forecasts were available here. [Keyword Planner forecast documentation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3022575?hl=en).

If the exact niche has too little search demand, broaden within the same buying problem, or shift effort to proof-led distribution. Do not silently broaden from “finish my app” to all AI curiosity just to buy more clicks.

## Start the blog from the delivery platform

The platform is the source of evidence. The article's subject should be the reader's decision or difficulty. “My harness architecture” can serve a technical audience; “My checks passed while the app was broken” connects the experience to a buyer worried about trusting their app.

Use a named author, show what was observed, distinguish the historical incident from a current demonstration, and label the work as PUNI's own project. Google recommends original, helpful material with firsthand experience and a clear audience; that guidance does not guarantee search rankings or client acquisition. [Google Search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

### First three articles

| Article                                                          | Evidence to use                                                                                                                                                              | Buyer takeaway                                                                         | Contextual invitation                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| My checks passed while my app was broken                         | The documented typecheck that compiled nothing and allowed a bad field to reach a running app; [incident catalogue](../findings/checks-that-cannot-fail.md#r5-catalogue-004) | A reassuring green check is useful only if it covers the behavior that matters         | “Unsure what your app's checks really prove? Tell us what you need to trust.” |
| Before inviting customers, try your app's most important journey | A real, explicitly scoped walkthrough from PUNI's own product; gather the demonstration before claiming an outcome                                                           | Follow the customer task end to end and record what succeeds and fails                 | “What is standing between your app and its first users?”                      |
| What happens when your next release goes wrong?                  | The recorded two-release website rollback and restore-write proof in [website verification](../../openspec/changes/puni-website-funnel/verify.md)                            | Being able to recover is a concrete delivery capability; make the evidence inspectable | “Need help making your next release less uncertain?”                          |

Lead with one incident, not a catalogue of every framework and tool. Keep the technical proof available for readers who want it, while explaining its customer consequence in ordinary language.

### First article outline

1. Open with the contradiction: a passing check and a real failure, explicitly identified as a documented incident in PUNI's own work.
2. Explain what the team assumed the check proved.
3. Show the specific missing coverage: the typecheck was compiling no files.
4. Explain the corrective idea: deliberately introduce the relevant fault and verify that the check catches it.
5. Give the reader one practical question: “What important failure would make this check turn red?”
6. State the limits: this improves evidence for the checked behavior; it does not make an app universally reliable.
7. Invite a relevant request through the same landing prompt and chat.

Possible opening, to verify against the final article evidence:

> A typecheck passed in our AI-assisted software project while compiling no files. A bad field then reached the running app. The useful question became: what would actually make this check fail?

### Distribution for every article

Produce one full article, a short clip or annotated image that shows the evidence, and a self-contained social post explaining the lesson. Adapt the post to one chosen audience venue rather than copying the same link everywhere. Answer relevant discussion questions with actual substance. A link to the related article or assessment is optional and must fit the venue.

Offer a contextual route back to the prompt near the article's useful conclusion. For a long article, a quiet earlier invitation is another test. Keep reading possible without surrendering an email. Measure whether readers start relevant requests, not only whether engineers share the post.

## Email capture and human follow-up are part of marketing

The [funnel proposal](conversion-funnel-options.md#email-request-and-submission) owns the submission copy. A request email should receive an acknowledgment and a thoughtful human reply with one useful next action. Set an internal response target the operator can sustain; do not publish a response deadline before arranging that coverage.

Proposed follow-up sequence for discussion: reply to the specific request; if unanswered, send one useful reminder after a few working days; then stop unless the prospect asks to continue. This cadence is a hypothesis, not a proven optimum. The topic remains the requested project. A recurring blog newsletter requires a separate invitation and is not counted as a project request.

Keep the first human step modest: clarify the blocker, propose the relevant review or build discussion, and make replying easy. A mandatory calendar booking after already giving an email adds another decision; offer it as an option.

## Define success before buying traffic

Use these working evaluation definitions, not new product-state contracts:

- **Submitted request:** the visitor explicitly sends a project request and a contact email. A chat email mention, sample download, or email-only subscription does not count.
- **Usable request:** a non-duplicate, non-spam submission whose description or selected answers identify a concrete service need or outcome and a usable-looking contact address. Delivery failure and lack of reply are reported separately; a syntactically plausible email is not proven contactability.
- **Qualified request:** human review finds plausible service fit and a real next conversation. Budget, urgency, and authority can remain unknown at this early stage; qualify consistently rather than inventing them from clicks.
- **Sales outcome:** prospect replied, discussion progressed, proposal offered, and paid work won. Preserve the distinction between these stages.

For each channel and campaign, report landing visits, chat starts, usable requests, qualified requests, replies, and paid engagements. The main near-term rate is usable requests divided by eligible landing visits. Also inspect usable requests per chat start to locate friction, and qualification rate to catch low-quality acquisition. Use comparable cohorts and time windows; later sales require time to mature.

Do not optimize a campaign for chat starts and assume that buys customers. Google's distinction between qualified and converted leads supports measuring later outcomes separately. [Google Ads lead-stage documentation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/11459091?hl=en).

## Economics without invented benchmarks

For paid traffic:

> Cost per usable request = campaign spend ÷ usable requests.
>
> Allowable acquisition cost per client = first-engagement contribution margin × the fraction allocated to acquisition.
>
> Allowable cost per usable request = allowable acquisition cost × the probability a usable request becomes a client.

Illustration only: €2,000 contribution margin, 25% allocated to acquisition, and a 10% usable-request-to-client rate imply €500 allowable acquisition cost and €50 per usable request. At an assumed 5% visit-to-usable-request rate, that corresponds to €2.50 per click. These inputs are not PUNI forecasts or industry benchmarks. Include follow-up and creative costs in the total acquisition decision; the simple CPC calculation isolates ad spend.

Start with a total spend cap and a learning question. When a campaign produces no requests, cost per request is undefined rather than zero. Reaching the cap means review and decide; it does not by itself prove the market is absent. Scale when the quality and economics support it, not because a cheap click rate looks encouraging.

## Experiment order

| Order | Comparison                                                                         | Hypothesis                                                        | Primary readout                                    | Guardrail                                       |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Current product categories vs outcome-oriented starters, within comparable traffic | Visitors recognize their need and enter with less uncertainty     | Usable requests per landing visit                  | Correct routing and request specificity         |
| 2     | Short request route vs useful recommendation before email                          | Added clarity earns enough trust to justify the extra interaction | Usable requests per landing visit                  | Completion time, qualification, and abandonment |
| 3     | Early voluntary contact vs email after summary for ready buyers                    | Ready visitors benefit from fewer mandatory questions             | Usable requests within the comparable intent group | Vague submissions and non-deliverable contacts  |
| 4     | Generic offer vs campaign-matched promise                                          | Continuity from ad to chat improves relevant requests             | Cost per qualified request                         | Total qualified volume and later replies        |
| 5     | Minimal proof near the prompt vs proof only farther down                           | Evidence resolves uncertainty before the first answer             | Usable requests per landing visit                  | Mobile clarity and distraction                  |
| 6     | Visual emphasis on “want” vs current presentation                                  | More attention reaches the starting action                        | Usable requests, with chat starts as a diagnostic  | Readability, motion comfort, and quality        |

Do not run every comparison at once. First observe representative people trying the journey and remove obvious confusion. Those sessions explain difficulties; they do not estimate conversion uplift. With sufficient traffic, randomize one important contrast at a time and set the primary outcome, meaningful effect size, observation window, and sample requirement before interpreting the result. Avoid comparing unrelated campaigns as though they were a randomized page test.

Controlled experimentation research explains why randomization, statistical power, and sample size matter. It supplies a method, not a universal visitor threshold. With sparse leads, report counts and uncertainty and treat changes as exploratory rather than announcing a winner. [Kohavi et al., Controlled experiments on the web, 2009](https://www.exp-platform.com/Documents/controlledExperimentDMKD.pdf).
